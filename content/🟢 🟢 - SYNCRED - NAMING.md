# SYNTHETIC - DEFINED

In common usage, **synthetic** refers to something that is produced by chemical synthesis (man-made) rather than occurring naturally. However, in your specific context, it leans into the word's Greek roots: _sun-_ (together) and _tithenai_ (to place).

When you "synthesize" something, you aren't just making it artificial; you are **integrating multiple diverse elements into a single, cohesive whole.**

### ANALYSIS OF "SYNCREDS"

Your portmanteau "SynCreds" is clever because it functions as a double entendre. It captures both the "modern/digital" nature of the tech and the "integrated" nature of the data.

Here is how your definition of "Synthetic" breaks down within that term:

| Element         | The "Synthesis" Role                                                                  |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| **Performance** | Provides **empirical proof** (e.g., links to code, sales data, or project outcomes).  |

### WHY "SYNCREDS" WORKS

1. **Multi-Dimensional:** Most Verifiable Credentials (VCs) are one-dimensional (Issuer \rightarrow Holder). Yours are 3D.
2. **Verification Synergy:** By "synthesizing" these three pillars, you create a credential that is much harder to fake or inflate than a standard digital certificate.
3. **Memorable:** It’s punchy and fits the "Web3/Identity" nomenclature.

### A SMALL NOTE ON PERCEPTION

In some technical circles (like AI or data science), **"Synthetic Data"** refers to data that is artificially generated by an algorithm rather than collected from real-world events.

Since your "SynCreds" are actually backed by **real** evidence and **real** peer reviews, you might occasionally want to clarify that "Synthetic" refers to the **synthesis of sources**, not that the credentials themselves are "fake" or "simulated."
